Paper Title:
Multiagent Finetuning: Self Improvement with Diverse Reasoning Chains

Authors & Year:
Vighnesh Subramaniam, Yilun Du, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Antonio Torralba, Shuang Li, Igor Mordatch — 2025

Link:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05707 (ICLR 2025)
Project page: https://llm-multiagent-ft.github.io

Summary:
A direct successor to Du et al.'s multi-agent debate: instead of fine-tuning a single self-improving model (which plateaus as response diversity collapses after 2–3 rounds), the authors fine-tune a *society* of models from the same base. Multi-agent debate generates the training data; each agent is then fine-tuned on independent subsets of data derived from its own responses and role, producing specialized generation and critic agents. This preserves diverse reasoning chains and sustains improvement across many more fine-tuning iterations than single-agent self-improvement, on frontier and open-source models (Phi-3, Mistral, LLaMA-3 8B, GPT-3.5). The finetuned agents also transfer zero-shot to new datasets.

Relevant to Our Idea:
This is the ICLR-published successor to the original MAD paper our Gate 0 reproduces, and it isolates *diversity collapse* as the bottleneck of multi-agent self-improvement — conceptually adjacent to Estornell & Liu's shared-misconception convergence and to our heterogeneity assumption (blueprint Assumption 4). It gives us a strong citation for "preserving reasoning diversity across agents matters," while also marking the boundary of our contribution: their fix is at training time, ours is a training-free inference-time trust mechanism. Their diversity metrics (consensus rate, KL divergence, embedding dissimilarity) are candidate instrumentation for our analysis chapter.

Gap / Limitation Noted in Paper:
The intervention requires fine-tuning each agent on interaction-generated data — incompatible with our training-free deployment constraint (heterogeneous served models, no gradient updates). It also never verifies correctness against external evidence: diverse reasoning chains can still converge on a diversely-reasoned but jointly wrong answer, which is exactly the failure class our evidence-grounded trust targets.

### 2. Core Contribution & Method

1. **Multi-agent debate as data generator** — agents produce answers across rounds; per-agent response sets become independent training data (the "correct output of model n after round m").
2. **Specialization by role** — distinct models are trained as *generation agents* (initial responses) and *critic agents* (evaluate/refine peers), creating a robust feedback loop.
3. **Independent data partitions** — each model is fine-tuned only on its own interaction data, preserving behavioral diversity rather than collapsing all models to one policy.
4. **Iterated finetuning** — the debate→finetune loop repeats; diversity is maintained across iterations where single-agent self-training collapses.
5. **Inference** — debate at inference time with majority voting over the specialized society.

**Diversity analysis (Appendix):** consensus rate, KL-divergence between agents, and embedding dissimilarity are used to show agents remain distinct as iterations progress.

### 4. Paper's Self-Admitted Limitations

- Repeated fine-tuning is expensive and unavailable for proprietary/API models (their GPT-3.5 experiments still require a fine-tuning endpoint).
- Improvement quality depends on debate-generated supervision; no ground-truth verification enters the loop.
- Long-run diversity is shown empirically, not theoretically bounded.
- No treatment of adversarial pressure, sycophancy, or trust calibration.

### 5. Direct Comparison to Our Idea

| Dimension | Multiagent Finetuning | Our Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervention point** | Training time (gradient updates per agent) | Inference time (no fine-tuning) |
| **Diversity strategy** | Independent data partitions + role specialization | Heterogeneous model families + source-partitioned retrieval |
| **Correctness signal** | None — self-generated data only | External evidence (PubMed/ArXiv/Semantic Scholar) |
| **Aggregation** | Majority voting after debate | Trust-weighted aggregation (evidence-calibrated) |
| **Resource profile** | Multiple fine-tuning runs | Served inference only (vLLM, A6000/A100) |
| **Failure it leaves open** | Jointly-wrong diverse consensus | Addressed by evidence-grounded trust |

### 6. Our Positioning Strategy

- **In our paper:** cite as the training-time branch of multi-agent self-improvement and the published evidence that diversity collapse drives plateauing — then contrast our training-free trust calibration.
- **How we cite:** "Diversity preservation has been pursued at training time (Subramaniam et al., 2025); we instead preserve effective diversity at inference time by weighting agents through externally verified evidence."
- **Pre-emptive rebuttal:** a reviewer may ask "why not fine-tune agents to be less sycophantic?" — this paper is the citation for *why that path exists*, while our Section 0 rejection (compute cost, no cross-family generalization, conflates sycophancy reduction with evidence grounding) explains why we did not take it.
