%The abstract should contain a summary of the work presented in the report in a single paragraph. The paragraph should provide a general background of the problem, methodology and results.

Multi-agent debate can improve the reliability of large language model answers on scientific questions, but a confident wrong majority can pressure a correct minority agent into abandoning the correct answer. This failure is called sycophantic collapse. Existing mitigations refine prompts or weight agents by self-reported uncertainty; we are not aware of a deployed method that combines claim-level external evidence with a bounded trust state that changes an agent's influence during the debate. This report develops a trust-calibrated deliberation framework in which three heterogeneous agents debate over three rounds, each response is decomposed into atomic claims, and each claim is checked against scientific literature retrieved for its domain. Supported and contradicted claims update a trust vector through logit accumulation, one softmax, and a simplex projection that enforces a floor and a sum-to-one constraint. The final answer is a trust-weighted plurality over the agents' final positions, not a head count. At the FYDP~II stage, the implementation is complete enough to run and test: the suite contains 523 passing tests (514 at the P0 release), the P0 gate reports 13 of 13 checks passed with 0 pending, and Gate 0 completed 10 of 10 vanilla debates with 90 of 90 non-empty positions at a mean of 370.8 seconds per debate. These results validate serving, orchestration, parsing, and the trust operator; they are not an efficacy result. The verifier human audit, the paid Core run, and the Final-stack smoke test remain to be completed, so no claim is made that the framework reduces collapse or improves accuracy.
